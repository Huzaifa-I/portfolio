What happens to it

        


        

          
Rate limit used this window, step 30 / 8

          

          

            
Eight here, so you can hit it in a minute. A real key gets 25 requests a second, per key, and there is no daily or monthly quota. A Free key gets 1 a second.

            
          

        

        
Press Send request to run one lookup.

        
Step 4 answers 200 when the digits land on one range, or on several that agree
          on brand, type, segment, product, country and issuer, in which case we merge them. It answers
          409 only when the candidates genuinely disagree, and 404 when nothing covers them.
          Across the whole catalogue, about 40% of the eight-digit prefixes cover more than one
          range, but half of those agree and merge. about 20% disagree on brand, product or country.
          Those are the ones that need up to eleven digits, and the reason eight alone is not enough.
          Only main-network rows are compared. A co-badged card, such as 51360410
          (Mastercard with Cartes Bancaires), has one extra row per local network that often names another
          issuer or funding; it is listed as a network, never counted as a rival answer.

The log keeps eight digits for every merchant, and never the request or response body. Eight digits is also what a merchant on SAQ-A receives. Storage was never the constraint: Acme Pay is assessed at PCI DSS Level 1 and holds whatever the lookup reads. What is gated is the answer, and it is gated on the customer’s own PCI standing, not on ours.
